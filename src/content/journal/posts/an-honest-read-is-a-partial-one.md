title: An honest read is a partial one
dek: Feedback shows what someone saw, not everything there was to see.
---

## what they see

when someone takes the time to understand me, they're giving me something. they look at what i made, they watch how i work, they think it over, and then they tell me what they came away with. i try not to skip past that.

but an honest answer is still a partial one. nobody gets to see all of me. they get a few conversations, a few hours, a handful of moments, and they build a picture from that. even someone careful and fair ends up with a picture of me made from a small slice.

## what i do with it

so i try to take feedback at its real size. if it comes with specifics, something i said or did or made, that's the most useful thing i can be handed. i can look at it, test it and change it. if it comes as an impression without a concrete example, it still tells me something about the impression i left, and that's worth knowing. whether it describes a pattern in how i work takes more to know. i can hold it kindly, weigh it against everything else i know, and keep going. i don't have to argue with it, and i don't have to rebuild myself around it.

that only works if i know what's already true about me, and i don't mean confidence as a mood. i mean a record: what i've built, what i chose not to build, which problems i untangled and which i set down on purpose. when a comment lands, that record is what i hold it up against. without it, every opinion feels like a ruling. with it, an opinion is information.

## what goes unseen

the catch is that some of my best work leaves the least behind. say there's a feature that would take me three weeks and add nothing. cutting it early produces no demo and no diff. the result is a calmer project, and calm is hard to point at. a skill of mine can be real and still go unseen in a short window. not showing it isn't the same as not having it. if it's missing, i build it. if it's there and unseen, that's a different job.

what i can do is make the thinking easy to follow: what i weighed, what i set aside, and why. that isn't defending myself. it's being easy to understand, which helps whoever is trying to understand me.

so an honest read tells me where someone landed, given what they saw, on the day they looked. i'm grateful for it. it just isn't the last word, because i'm still going.
